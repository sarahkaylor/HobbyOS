GB-238 — DR0/DR7 + last-#DB instrument for the shape-(b) miss
============================================================

Head: 9ba0a72dafc3249f1bee2d3766dbda956248163b (GB-234 head, stacked on
      ticket/146a-badvec 90e5d3a7) + the GB-238 commit on this branch.
Worktree: /home/sarah/Documents/GitHub/HobbyOS/.worktrees/gb238
SW-VM: swarm-farm-3 (verifier, debug reserve — gaming.md §5.1).

Card question (GB-238 #591): GB-234-V (#587) measured the DR0 watch armed on
the exact changed slot (`slot=0x25FFB748`, `armed=1`) with zero `[BADVEC] writer`
rows. This card asks why the #DB never reported, with three named hypotheses:
(1) DR0/DR7 clobbered before the write, (2) the #DB fired but its record was
suppressed, (3) the write happened where DR7's L0 was clear.

What this branch adds (src/kernel/arch/x64/trap.c)
--------------------------------------------------
1. Every fatal `[BADVEC]` dump now prints the LIVE debug registers of the CPU
   taking the dump and the owner CPU the entry stamp named:

     [BADVEC] drstate cpu=<dumper> owner_cpu=<stamp cpu> dr0=<live> dr7=<live>
              dr6=<live> now_ms=<timer_get_ms>

   followed by one `[BADVEC] lastdb` row per CPU that ever armed a watch or took
   a #DB: arm_ms, db_ms, db_pc, db_dr6, result, disarms, disarm_ms, disarm_pc,
   slot, armed.
2. Every #DB (`tf->vector == 1`) records its time, RIP, DR6 and how it was
   treated, and prints one bounded `[BADVEC] dbg` row (max 16):
   `printed=yes|no` and `why=dr0-hit | dr6-no-dr0-bit | no-armed-watch-on-this-cpu`.
3. Arm/disarm record their own timestamps; disarm records the #DB RIP. The
   existing writer/consume behaviour is unchanged (observation only).
4. tools/gate/panic_sink_allowlist_test.py: trap.c 236 -> 294 references.

Interpretation of the new row (printed by the dump, not inferred):
- DR7 bit 0 (L0) = 1 and DR0 == the changed vector_slot at the fatal dump:
  the hardware watch was still armed; a clobber is excluded for that epoch.
- db_ms == 0 with arm_ms set and disarms == 0: no #DB was ever delivered on
  that CPU after the arm.
- db_ms > arm_ms: a #DB was delivered; `result` says whether it was printed
  (1) or consumed without DR6.B0 (2).
- disarms > 0 with disarm_pc: the watch was consumed at that RIP.

Instrument validation (not the gate wave)
-----------------------------------------
A separate 120 s boot at the same head took two kernel-mode vector=13 fatals
whose slots were UNCHANGED, and printed the new rows verbatim:

  [BADVEC] fatal frame=0x0000000023FFB6B0 in_user=1 vector_slot=0x0000000023FFB7B8 cs_slot=0x0000000023FFB7C8
  [BADVEC] entry slots unchanged: frame=0x0000000023FFB6B0 v=13 cs=0x0000000000000008 entry_pid=15 cpu=6
    [BADVEC] drstate cpu=6 owner_cpu=6 dr0=0x0000000023FFB7B8 dr7=0x0000000000090401 dr6=0x00000000FFFF0FF0 now_ms=0x0000000000002401
    [BADVEC] lastdb cpu=4 arm_ms=0x00000000000023F8 db_ms=0x0000000000000000 db_pc=0x0000000000000000 db_dr6=0x0000000000000000 result=0 disarms=0 disarm_ms=0x0000000000000000 disarm_pc=0x0000000000000000 slot=0x0000000023FFB6D8 armed=1
    [BADVEC] lastdb cpu=6 arm_ms=0x00000000000023F8 db_ms=0x0000000000000000 db_pc=0x0000000000000000 db_dr6=0x0000000000000000 result=0 disarms=0 disarm_ms=0x0000000000000000 disarm_pc=0x0000000000000000 slot=0x0000000023FFB7B8 armed=1
  [KERNEL] FATAL: Exception in Kernel Mode! Vector: 13 Error Code: 0x0000000000000000

DR7=0x0000000000090401 decodes as L0=1 (bit 0), RW0=01 write (bit 16),
LEN0=10 8-byte (bit 19), plus the mandatory reserved bit 10 (0x400): the watch
was armed and DR0 equals the frame's vector slot. DR6=0xFFFF0FF0 has bit 0
clear (no DR0 hit), db_ms=0 and disarms=0 — the record states "never delivered
on this CPU" exactly as the card's falsifier requires. Because these frames'
slots were unchanged, this validates the instrument path, not the GB-234 miss.
